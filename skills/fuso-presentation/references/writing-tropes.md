# AI writing tropes check

Run this on the header and the text of every figure before rendering. This file is the check; it does not depend on any other skill being installed. The figure must read as if a member of the team typed it, so anything on this list is rewritten or deleted.

## How to check

1. Read the text sentence by sentence. For each sentence ask: does it state a fact from the post or from the publication, or is it only there for effect or to connect two other sentences? Delete the ones that state no fact, then join what is left.
2. Go through the lists below and rewrite every match in plain words.
3. Check every claim against the post. Do not add claims, stakes or conclusions the post does not contain, and do not state them more strongly than the post does.
4. Read the header and the text aloud once. If a sentence sounds like a slogan, a summary line or a commentator, rewrite it as a plain statement.
5. Check the lengths again after rewriting.

## Sentence patterns

* Bridge and commentary sentences that state no fact: "That settles the question for employment.", "The rule covers one state and one kind of decision.", "Such rules have few precedents.", "And that matters.", "This is new territory."
* Hooks that address the reader or dramatise the topic: "Your chatbot can ...", "Ever wondered ...", "Imagine ...", "What if ...".
* Short fragments for punch: "Useful, and a privacy risk.", "No cameras. No microphones."
* Contrast frames: "not X, but Y", "It's not just X, it's Y", "more than just X", "by design, not by accident".
* Parallel pairs and lists of three for rhythm: "one state and one kind of decision", "designed, auditable and contestable".
* Colon or dash reveals: "The result: ...", "The catch? ...", a dash before a dramatic ending.
* A question the text then answers itself.
* Sweeping claims: "this settles ...", "no law ever ...", "the first", "never", "always", "everyone".
* A closing moral, takeaway or invitation: "A law worth watching", "Read more below".
* Announcing what the text is about to do: "Here's what this means", "Let's take a look".
* "From X to Y" ranges and "Whether you are X or Y, ..." that are not in the post.

## Words and phrases

* Inflated verbs for "says" or "is about": unpacks, sheds light on, dives into, takes a deep dive, explores, delves into, tackles, navigates, underscores, highlights.
* Stiff filler: it is worth noting, in this context, when it comes to, plays a role in, raises questions about, at its core.
* Enthusiasm: excited, thrilled, fascinating, exciting, insightful, thought-provoking, groundbreaking, game changer.
* Vocabulary: landscape, realm, tapestry, crucial, pivotal, key (as an adjective), leverage, foster, unlock, robust, seamless, cutting-edge, at the intersection of, in today's world, increasingly.

## Formatting

* No dashes of any kind as punctuation. No bold or italic Unicode letters.
* No emojis, hashtags, links or exclamation marks.
